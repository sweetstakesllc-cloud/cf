Authenticity

If it isn't genuine, it isn't here. Every piece is inspected in our studio in Sweden before it is photographed, priced or listed.

Step 01

Physical Inspection

Every item arrives at the studio. Nothing is listed from photographs alone, and we never sell an item we haven't held.

Step 02

Verification

Hardware, stitching, lining, date codes and serials are checked against the maker's known references for the era the piece is from.

Step 03

Condition Grading

We grade honestly and photograph the wear rather than hiding it. What you see on the listing is what arrives.

What We Check

  • Hardware — weight, finish, engraving depth and the way zips and clasps move.
  • Stitching — stitch count, angle and thread colour against the reference for that model and year.
  • Materials — grain, coating, canvas print alignment and how the leather has aged.
  • Date codes and serials — format, placement and whether they're consistent with the item's construction.
  • Interior — lining fabric, stamps and pocket construction, which are often where copies fall short.

Our Guarantee

If an item bought from Circular Fash is ever found not to be genuine, we refund the full purchase price. No time limit, no conditions about how you found out.

That guarantee is the whole business. Pre-owned luxury only works if the buyer can trust the seller, so we would rather turn an item away than take a risk on it.

We turn down items every week. It costs us sales, and it is the reason the ones we do list are worth buying.

Condition Grades

We use three grades. They describe wear, not age — a twenty-year-old bag that has been stored well can grade higher than a two-year-old one carried daily.

  • Excellent — barely worn. Minimal signs of use, no structural wear, hardware bright.
  • Very Good — light, even signs of wear consistent with careful use. Nothing that affects how the piece functions.
  • Good — visible wear such as patina, softening or light marks. Structurally sound and ready to be worn.

Every listing spells out what the grade means for that specific item, and the photographs show it.
